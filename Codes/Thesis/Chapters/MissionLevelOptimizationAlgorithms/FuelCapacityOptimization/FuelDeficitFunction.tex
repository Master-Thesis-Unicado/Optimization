The fuel deficit quantifies the shortfall or surplus relative to consumption requirements:

\begin{equation}
\text{Deficit} = F_{consumed} - F_{available}
\end{equation}

A positive deficit indicates insufficient fuel capacity, while a negative deficit represents fuel surplus. The optimization seeks to minimize the absolute deficit while ensuring non-negative capacity:

\begin{equation}
\min_{F_{available}} |\text{Deficit}| \quad \text{subject to } \text{Deficit} \leq 0
\end{equation}

By updating bounds according to the deficit sign, the bisection method upholds this constraint and guarantees that the converged solution represents the lowest fuel capacity that meets mission requirements. The deficit calculation is performed for each iteration, providing  information regarding fuel sufficiency at each test point in the optimization process.
