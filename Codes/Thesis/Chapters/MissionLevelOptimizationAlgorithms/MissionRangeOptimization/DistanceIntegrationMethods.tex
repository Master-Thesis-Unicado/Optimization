Distance computation for each phase proceeds through integration of true airspeed over time. For climb and descent phases, the distance is computed from the optimized trajectory:

\begin{equation}
s_{climb} = \int_{0}^{t_{climb}} V_{TAS}(t) \, dt \approx \sum_{i} M_i \cdot a(h_i) \cdot \Delta t_i
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
s_{descent} = \int_{0}^{t_{descent}} V_{TAS}(t) \, dt \approx \sum_{i} M_i \cdot a(h_i) \cdot \Delta t_i
\end{equation}

where the small-angle approximation assumes that horizontal velocity approximately equals true airspeed for typical climb and descent angles.

For cruise, distance is determined directly from the specified cruise distance parameter, as level cruise maintains constant true airspeed:

\begin{equation}
s_{cruise} = V_{TAS} \cdot t_{cruise} = M_{cruise} \cdot a(h_{cruise}) \cdot t_{cruise}
\end{equation}
