
Grid resolution selection requires balancing solution accuracy against computational cost. The $15 \times 15 \times 15$ grid provides rapid computation (4.9 minutes) but exhibits discretization errors affecting trajectory optimality, with fuel consumption 13.2\% higher than the finest grid and poor cruise Mach constraint satisfaction (error of 0.020). The $55 \times 55 \times 55$ grid offers highest accuracy with excellent terminal constraint satisfaction and optimal fuel consumption (12,727.0 kg) but requires substantial computational resources (33.5 minutes, representing a 6.84-fold increase).

Intermediate grids such as $30 \times 30 \times 30$ or $35 \times 35 \times 35$ provide optimal accuracy-computation trade-offs, with solution quality approaching that of fine-grid results at significantly reduced computational cost. Grid density 30 achieves fuel consumption of 13,236.9 kg (within 4.0\% of the finest grid) with excellent cruise Mach constraint satisfaction (error of 0.001) at 9.5 minutes computation time. Grid density 35 achieves fuel consumption of 13,049.5 kg (within 2.5\% of the finest grid) with good constraint satisfaction (errors less than 0.004) at 11.5 minutes computation time.

Convergence analysis indicates that fuel consumption changes between successive grid resolutions decrease as resolution increases, with marginal improvements above grid density 35 typically less than 1\%, suggesting asymptotic approach toward the continuous optimal solution. The analysis recommends grid densities of 30-35 for routine mission analysis, providing a balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. Finer grids (40-55) should be reserved for high-precision studies where terminal constraint accuracy is critical or where the additional computational cost is justified by the accuracy requirements.
