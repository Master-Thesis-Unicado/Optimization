
The center of gravity analysis quantifies longitudinal CG movement throughout the optimized mission under the INNER\_FIRST fuel consumption scenario, following the fuel consumption scenarios and depletion strategies introduced in Section~\ref{par:fuel-consumption-scenarios}. This analysis evaluates aircraft stability and trim characteristics as fuel is consumed, with particular focus on CG travel magnitude and its relationship to the sequential tank depletion strategy.

The aircraft's longitudinal center of gravity shifts aft continuously as fuel is consumed, with initial position $x_{\text{CG},0} = 16.057$ m increasing to final position $x_{\text{CG},f} = 16.207$ m at mission completion. The total CG travel amounts to $\Delta x_{\text{CG}} = 0.150$ m, representing the aft shift from maximum fuel loading to mission completion. The zero-fuel CG position, corresponding to complete fuel depletion, is $x_{\text{CG},\text{zero}} = 16.208$ m. The final mission CG lies $0.001$ m forward of the zero-fuel position, confirming that a fuel reserve of $615.8$ kg remains in the outer tanks at mission completion.

The CG evolution exhibits an S-curve characteristic with three distinct segments corresponding to sequential tank depletion. During the initial phase ($0$--$4,000$ kg fuel consumed), the CG increases from $16.057$ m to approximately $16.115$ m as the center tank is depleted. The second phase ($4,000$--$8,500$ kg fuel consumed) shows continued CG increase from $16.115$ m to approximately $16.20$ m as inner tanks are consumed. The final phase ($8,500$--$12,663$ kg) exhibits a plateau where the CG stabilizes around $16.20$--$16.207$ m as outer tanks are depleted. This piecewise behavior reflects the sequential nature of the INNER\_FIRST fuel management strategy, where each tank group is depleted completely before the next phase begins.

The fuel tank evolution, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:cg_tank_evolution}, demonstrates the INNER\_FIRST sequential consumption strategy. Initial fuel distribution comprises approximately $4,800$ kg in outer tanks, $4,500$ kg in inner tanks, and $4,000$ kg in the center tank, totaling approximately $13,300$ kg initial fuel capacity. The tank depletion follows a strictly sequential pattern characteristic of the INNER\_FIRST strategy, where each tank group is fully depleted before consumption begins from the next group.

During the first consumption phase ($0$--$4,000$ kg fuel consumed), the center tank depletes linearly from $4,000$ kg to $0$ kg while outer and inner tanks remain at full capacity. The second phase ($4,000$--$8,500$ kg fuel consumed) depletes the inner tanks from $4,500$ kg to $0$ kg while the outer tanks remain constant at approximately $4,800$ kg. The final phase ($8,500$--$12,663$ kg fuel consumed) consumes fuel from the outer tanks, reducing them from $4,800$ kg to the reserve level of approximately $615.8$ kg ($307.9$ kg per side). This sequential depletion pattern ensures controlled CG movement while maintaining lateral symmetry through balanced left-right consumption within each tank group.

The fuel tank center of gravity evolution, presented in Figure~\ref{fig:cg_fuel_tank_cg}, demonstrates that the fuel's center of gravity varies significantly throughout the mission as a consequence of the sequential tank depletion strategy. The fuel CG begins at approximately $x_{\text{CG},\text{fuel}} = 15.35$ m with all tanks loaded and increases monotonically throughout fuel consumption, reaching approximately $x_{\text{CG},\text{fuel}} = 16.07$ m at mission completion when only outer tank fuel remains.

This fuel CG variation reflects the sequential INNER\_FIRST consumption logic combined with the different longitudinal positions of the fuel tanks. The center tank is located at $x_{\text{CG}} = 14.99$ m (most forward), inner tanks at $x_{\text{CG}} = 14.68$ m (forward), and outer tanks at $x_{\text{CG}} = 16.09$ m (most aft). As the center tank (forward) is depleted first, the fuel CG shifts aft. Subsequently, as inner tanks (also forward) are depleted, the fuel CG continues shifting aft toward the outer tank position, reaching $16.07$ m when only outer tank fuel remains at mission completion. The monotonically increasing fuel CG trajectory contrasts with the S-curve aircraft CG evolution, demonstrating how the sequential depletion of forward-positioned tanks progressively shifts the remaining fuel mass aft.

The aircraft CG range analysis quantifies the total CG travel and operational limits. The minimum CG position occurs at mission start, $x_{\text{CG},\min} = 16.057$ m, corresponding to maximum fuel loading. The maximum CG position occurs at mission completion, $x_{\text{CG},\max} = 16.207$ m, representing the aft-most CG position during the mission. The total CG travel of $\Delta x_{\text{CG}} = 0.150$ m represents the aft shift from initial to final CG positions. The zero-fuel CG position, $x_{\text{CG},\text{zero}} = 16.208$ m, represents the theoretical maximum aft CG position if all fuel were consumed. The final mission CG ($16.207$ m) is $0.001$ m forward of the zero-fuel CG, reflecting the $615.8$ kg fuel reserve remaining in the outer tanks at mission completion.

The CG travel of $0.150$ m represents a relatively small displacement, indicating that the fuel management strategy effectively controls CG movement. This limited travel ensures stability margins remain within acceptable limits throughout the mission, maintaining handling characteristics and minimizing trim drag variations. The analysis confirms that the INNER\_FIRST strategy successfully balances fuel efficiency objectives with stability and control requirements, with the final CG position ($16.207$ m) remaining well within the aircraft's certified CG envelope. The sequential tank depletion (center $\rightarrow$ inner $\rightarrow$ outer) produces an S-curve aircraft CG evolution and a monotonically increasing fuel CG trajectory, demonstrating effective CG management through controlled fuel consumption sequencing.
