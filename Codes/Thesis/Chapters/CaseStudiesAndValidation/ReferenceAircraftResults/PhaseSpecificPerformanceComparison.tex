Table~\ref{tab:phase_comparison} provides detailed phase-specific performance metrics for both reference and optimized missions. The climb phase demonstrates the most significant optimization benefits, with fuel consumption reduced by $71.0\%$ and climb rate increased by $126\%$ ($632$ m/min versus $279$ m/min), enabling faster altitude gain with substantially reduced fuel consumption. The optimized climb achieves a higher average lift-to-drag ratio ($16.0$ versus $10.8$) and lower average drag coefficient ($0.036$ versus $0.055$), reflecting improved aerodynamic efficiency through trajectory optimization. The optimized climb operates at a higher average velocity ($245$ m/s versus $160$ m/s) with lower average thrust ($95,000$ N versus $130,000$ N) and lower throttle setting ($77\%$ versus $85\%$), indicating that the optimization algorithm selects more efficient climb profiles that require less thrust while achieving better performance.

The cruise phase shows increased fuel consumption ($11,465.6$ kg versus $9,752.8$ kg) but achieves a $26.1\%$ increase in cruise distance ($3,757.0$ km versus $2,979.2$ km). The optimized cruise operates at a higher velocity ($240.0$ m/s versus $233.6$ m/s) and Mach number ($0.80$ versus $0.78$) with lower average thrust ($42,000$ N versus $48,816$ N) and lower throttle setting ($72\%$ versus $85\%$), achieving a higher average lift-to-drag ratio ($15.0$ versus $11.8$) with lower drag coefficient ($0.036$ versus $0.0385$), demonstrating the optimization algorithm's ability to exploit more efficient operating points that require less thrust for the same or better performance.

The descent phase achieves the largest relative fuel savings, with fuel consumption reduced by $79.7\%$ ($117.3$ kg versus $579.3$ kg). Both missions employ idle throttle ($0\%$) during descent, but the optimized descent achieves a significantly higher descent rate ($1,839$ m/min versus $375$ m/min), reducing descent duration by $81.4\%$ while maintaining fuel efficiency. The optimized descent operates at a higher average velocity ($180$ m/s versus $150$ m/s) with zero thrust ($0$ N versus $5,000$ N) and achieves improved aerodynamic characteristics with lower drag coefficient ($0.036$ versus $0.045$).
