Table~\ref{tab:phase_comparison} provides detailed phase-specific performance metrics for both reference and optimized missions. The climb phase demonstrates the most significant optimization benefits, with fuel consumption reduced by $70.8\%$ and climb rate increased by $249\%$ ($617$ m/min versus $177$ m/min), enabling faster altitude gain with substantially reduced fuel consumption. The optimized climb achieves a higher average lift-to-drag ratio ($18.2$ versus $11.5$) with a slightly higher average drag coefficient ($0.045$ versus $0.039$), reflecting improved aerodynamic efficiency through trajectory optimization. The optimized climb operates at a lower average velocity ($153$ m/s versus $213$ m/s) but achieves a significantly higher climb rate ($615$ m/min versus $177$ m/min), indicating that the optimization algorithm selects steeper climb profiles that prioritize rapid altitude gain over forward speed, reducing climb duration by $74\%$ while achieving $70.8\%$ fuel savings.

The cruise phase shows increased fuel consumption ($11,405.6$ kg versus $9,752.8$ kg) but achieves a $26.1\%$ increase in cruise distance ($3,757.0$ km versus $2,979.2$ km). The optimized cruise operates at a constant velocity ($230.5$ m/s versus $233.6$ m/s) and Mach number ($0.77$ versus $0.78$) with lower average thrust ($49,300$ N versus $48,816$ N) and lower throttle setting ($71\%$-$73\%$ versus $85\%$), achieving a higher average lift-to-drag ratio ($13.6$ versus $11.8$) with comparable drag coefficient ($0.038$ versus $0.0385$), demonstrating the simulation's ability to maintain efficient operating points throughout the cruise segment.

The descent phase achieves the largest relative fuel savings, with fuel consumption reduced by $79.6\%$ ($118.4$ kg versus $579.3$ kg). Both missions employ idle throttle ($0\%$) during descent, but the optimized descent achieves a significantly higher descent rate ($1,353$ m/min versus $305$ m/min), reducing descent duration by $77.4\%$ while maintaining fuel efficiency. The optimized descent operates at a higher average velocity ($180$ m/s versus $107$ m/s) with minimal thrust. The reference descent exhibits higher average L/D ratio ($14.9$ versus $7.6$) but also higher drag coefficient ($0.117$ versus $0.043$), reflecting different descent strategies between the two missions.
