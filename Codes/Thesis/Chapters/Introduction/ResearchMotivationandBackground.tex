
The aviation industry faces escalating pressures to reduce operational costs and environmental impact. Fuel consumption represents a significant portion of airline operating expenses, typically accounting for 20-30\%~\cite{} of total operating costs for commercial carriers. Furthermore, aviation emissions contribute substantially to global greenhouse gas inventories, with international air transport emissions continuing to grow despite technological improvements.

Traditional mission analysis methods rely on static specific excess power (SEP) diagrams to guide trajectory planning. While these diagrams provide intuitive visual representations that assist designers in understanding performance characteristics, they suffer from a fundamental limitation: the entire operational profile is computed assuming a fixed operational mass. This assumption introduces significant inaccuracies, as fuel consumption throughout the mission continuously alters aircraft mass, thereby affecting performance parameters and trajectory characteristics. The resulting discrepancy between assumed and actual mass leads to suboptimal trajectory selections that fail to account for dynamic mass evolution.

Additionally, conventional design approaches treat mission range as an emergent outcome of fuel capacity and trajectory selection. In contrast, this work positions range as an explicit design criterion, enabling mission analysis procedures that directly target specified range requirements rather than accepting range as a consequence of other design choices. This paradigm shift facilitates more flexible design space exploration where range constraints drive optimization rather than emerge from it.

Trajectory optimization offers a direct pathway to reducing fuel consumption through intelligent flight path planning. By systematically exploring alternative trajectories and selecting those that minimize fuel consumption while satisfying operational constraints, optimization-based methods can achieve measurable improvements over conventional operational procedures. Studies have demonstrated that optimal trajectory planning can reduce fuel consumption by 3-8\%~\cite{} compared to standard operational profiles, translating to substantial cost savings and emission reductions when applied across airline fleets.

The significance of trajectory optimization extends beyond individual flight efficiency to broader system-level considerations. As air traffic management systems evolve toward trajectory-based operations, the ability to compute optimal trajectories becomes increasingly important for both strategic and tactical planning. Integration with air traffic control systems requires robust optimization algorithms capable of generating feasible trajectories under diverse operational scenarios and constraint sets.

Traditional mission analysis approaches have historically optimized flight phases independently, assuming fixed initial and terminal conditions for each phase. While this simplification facilitates computational tractability, it inherently leads to suboptimal global solutions. The coupling between phases through mass evolution, energy state, and operational continuity requirements necessitates integrated optimization frameworks that consider complete mission profiles as unified decision problems.
