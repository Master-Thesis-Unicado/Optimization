
Mission analysis for aircraft operations requires determination of flight trajectories that satisfy mission objectives while operating within physical and operational constraints. The fundamental challenge involves identifying trajectories that minimize fuel consumption subject to constraints including flight envelope limits, terminal state requirements, and operational procedures.

Conventional mission analysis methods employ phase-by-phase optimization strategies that treat climb, cruise, and descent as independent optimization problems. This approach introduces suboptimality through two primary mechanisms. First, the terminal conditions for one phase become fixed initial conditions for the subsequent phase, preventing exploitation of phase transition trade-offs. Second, mass evolution throughout the mission creates coupling between phases that cannot be properly captured when phases are optimized separately.

The problem addressed in this work encompasses development of an integrated mission analysis framework that simultaneously optimizes climb, cruise, and descent trajectories while accounting for dynamic mass evolution operational and environmental constraints. The framework must handle multi-dimensional state spaces including altitude, Mach number, and throttle lever position, which requires sophisticated optimization algorithms capable of managing high-dimensional decision spaces while maintaining computational tractability.

Operational constraints introduce additional complexity. Flight envelope limits including maximum operating Mach number, stall boundaries, and engine limits define feasible regions that vary with altitude and aircraft mass. Terminal constraints require achieving specific altitude and Mach number targets at phase transitions, while maintaining operational viability throughout the trajectory. Throttle lever limitations reflect engine operational regimes with different time limits and wear considerations, further constraining the optimization space.

The computational challenge involves solving a high-dimensional, non-linear optimization problem with complex constraint sets. Dynamic programming provides a framework for addressing this challenge through systematic exploration of the state space, but requires careful formulation to balance solution quality with computational cost.
