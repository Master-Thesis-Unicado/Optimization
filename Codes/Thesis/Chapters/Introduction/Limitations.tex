Several limitations and assumptions constrain the applicability of the developed methodology:

\textbf{Atmospheric Modeling:} The framework employs standard atmospheric models (ISA) and does not account for weather effects, wind variations, or atmospheric disturbances. The optimization assumes static atmospheric conditions throughout mission execution.

\textbf{Aircraft Modeling:} The methodology employs point-mass aircraft dynamics, neglecting rotational dynamics, structural flexibility, and detailed aerodynamic interactions. Aerodynamic characteristics are represented through simplified models that may not capture all nonlinear effects present in actual flight operations.

\textbf{Operational Constraints:} The framework incorporates a subset of operational constraints relevant to trajectory optimization. Additional constraints such as airspace restrictions, traffic separation requirements, and air traffic control procedures are not explicitly modeled.

\textbf{Computational Considerations:} The dynamic programming approach requires discretization of continuous state spaces, introducing approximation errors that depend on grid resolution. Computational requirements scale with state space dimensionality and discretization density, potentially limiting applicability to very high-fidelity scenarios requiring extremely fine resolution.
