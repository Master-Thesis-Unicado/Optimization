
Aircraft trajectory optimization represents a fundamental challenge in modern aviation operations, balancing competing objectives of fuel efficiency, operational constraints, and mission requirements. The field encompasses numerous optimization approaches, ranging from simplified analytical methods to highly detailed numerical frameworks. However, this diversity presents a critical trade-off: as optimization models incorporate increasing levels of detail and fidelity, their computational demands grow substantially, limiting practical applicability in scenarios requiring rapid iteration and evaluation.

This thesis develops a mission analysis system employing three-dimensional dynamic programming for trajectory optimization across all flight phases, specifically designed for concept design applications. The framework addresses the challenge of achieving sufficient accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for fast iteration cycles, where numerous mission configurations must be evaluated rapidly during the design process. The integrated multi-phase optimization accounts for dynamic mass evolution, atmospheric variations, and operational constraints throughout complete mission profiles, enabling efficient exploration of design spaces without compromising solution quality.

\textbf{Three-Dimensional Dynamic Programming Implementation:} Extend classical two-dimensional trajectory optimization approaches to incorporate throttle lever position as an explicit optimization variable, enabling simultaneous optimization of altitude, Mach number, and thrust schedules. This extension requires formulation of a three-dimensional state space with appropriate discretization strategies and transition kernels that maintain computational tractability while preserving solution quality.

\textbf{Integrated Multi-Phase Optimization:} Develop optimization procedures for climb, cruise, and descent phases that account for phase coupling through mass evolution and state continuity. The framework must enable seamless integration of optimized climb and descent trajectories with cruise simulation, ensuring physical consistency across phase transitions and enabling global mission-level optimization.

\textbf{Mission-Level Optimization Algorithms:} Implement higher-level optimization procedures for mission planning objectives including fuel capacity minimization and range targeting. These algorithms operate on complete mission simulations, iteratively adjusting mission parameters to achieve specified objectives while maintaining feasibility and optimality.

\textbf{Dynamic Mass and Center of Gravity Modeling:} Incorporate dynamic mass evolution throughout mission profiles, accounting for fuel consumption effects on aircraft performance, trim characteristics, and center of gravity position. The implementation must capture coupling between mass evolution and trajectory optimization through predictor-corrector integration schemes.

\textbf{Validation and Performance Assessment:} Demonstrate the effectiveness of the developed methodology through comprehensive case studies, sensitivity analyses, and comparisons with conventional trajectory planning methods. The validation must quantify fuel savings potential, assess computational performance, and evaluate solution quality metrics including constraint satisfaction and terminal condition accuracy.

These objectives collectively address the need for integrated mission analysis tools that bridge the gap between theoretical optimization methods and practical operational requirements, providing a foundation for enhanced trajectory planning capabilities in commercial aviation applications.
