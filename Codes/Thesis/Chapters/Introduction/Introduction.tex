
Aircraft trajectory optimization represents a fundamental challenge in modern aviation operations, balancing competing objectives of fuel efficiency, operational constraints, and mission requirements. The field encompasses numerous optimization approaches, ranging from simplified analytical methods to highly detailed numerical frameworks. However, this diversity presents a critical trade-off: as optimization models incorporate increasing levels of detail and fidelity, their computational demands grow substantially, limiting practical applicability in scenarios requiring rapid iteration and evaluation.

This thesis develops a mission analysis system employing three-dimensional dynamic programming for trajectory optimization across all flight phases, specifically designed for UNICADO, conceptual aircraft design environment. The framework addresses the challenge of achieving sufficient accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for fast iteration cycles, where numerous mission configurations must be evaluated rapidly during the design process. The integrated multi-phase optimization accounts for dynamic mass evolution, atmospheric variations, and operational constraints throughout complete mission profiles, enabling efficient exploration of design spaces without compromising solution quality.
