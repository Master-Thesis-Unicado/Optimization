The force balance equations govern the motion of the aircraft point mass. In the vertical plane, the equilibrium of forces yields relationships between thrust $T$, drag $D$, lift $L$, and weight $W = mg$:

\begin{equation}
T - D = m\frac{dV}{dt} + mg\sin\gamma
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
L - W\cos\gamma = mV\frac{d\gamma}{dt}
\end{equation}

Where, \(V\) is the true airspeed, \(\gamma\) is the flight-path angle, \(m\) the aircraft mass, and \(g\) the gravitational acceleration. In climb or descent, \(\gamma\) links directly to the altitude rate through
\[\sin\gamma = \frac{1}{V}\frac{dh}{dt}.\]
