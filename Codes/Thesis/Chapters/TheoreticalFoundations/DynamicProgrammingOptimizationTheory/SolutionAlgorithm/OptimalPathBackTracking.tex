After the forward pass completes, the optimal trajectory is recovered by backtracking from the terminal state to the initial state. The matrix \(\text{prv}[k,i,j]\) stores the index pair of the state that leads to \((k,i,j)\) with the lowest cost of transition,  which allows the path to be traced back step by step. 

The backtracking procedure begins at the terminal state $(K-1, i^*, j^*)$ determined by:

\begin{equation}
(i^*, j^*) = \arg\min_{(i,j)} F[K-1, i, j]
\end{equation}


Backtracking then follows predecessor links:

\begin{equation}
(k-1, i_{k-1}, j_{k-1}) = \text{prv}[k, i_k, j_k]
\end{equation}

continuing until reaching the initial stage $k = 0$. The sequence of states $(h_0, M_0, \delta_0) \to (h_1, M_1, \delta_1) \to \ldots \to (h_{K-1}, M_{K-1}, \delta_{K-1})$ represents the optimal trajectory.
