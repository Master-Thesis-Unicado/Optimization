\subsubsection{Maximum Operating Mach Number}
Several operational constraints in the model limit the feasible flight envelope.. The maximum operating Mach number \(M_{MMO}\) is the highest Mach at which the aircraft can operate safely, as specified by the engine model.

\[
M_{MMO} = 0.94
\]

\subsubsection{Maximum Service Ceiling}
The maximum service ceiling \(M_{MSC}\) defines the upper altitude limit allowed by the engine model. In the current implementation, this value is fixed.

\[
M_{MSC} = 13\,994.1 \,\text{m}
\]


\subsubsection{Stall Mach Number} 
Stall boundaries establish minimum speeds based on the maximum lift coefficient $C_{L,max}$ and current aircraft mass:

\begin{equation}
V_{stall} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S C_{L,max}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2mg}{\rho(h) S C_{L,max}}}
\end{equation}

In Mach number form, the minimum safe Mach becomes:

\begin{equation}
M_{min} = \frac{V_{stall}}{a(h)} = \sqrt{\frac{2mg}{\rho(h) S C_{L,max} a^2(h)}}
\end{equation}

The implementation computes stall Mach dynamically, which incorporates a safety margin factor $k_{safety}$ such that $M_{min} = M_{stall} \times k_{safety}$ to ensure operational clearance above the theoretical stall boundary. This safety margin is set to 1.3, providing a 30\% buffer above the stall speed.
