This thesis presents a comprehensive methodology for aircraft trajectory optimization using dynamic programming techniques. The research addresses the challenge of determining minimum-fuel flight trajectories while satisfying operational constraints and mission requirements.

The methodology employs a three-dimensional state space formulation incorporating altitude, velocity, and mass as state variables. Dynamic programming algorithms are implemented to optimize climb, cruise, and descent phases, with mission-level optimization procedures addressing fuel capacity minimization and range targeting objectives.

Key contributions include the development of a unified optimization framework that accounts for atmospheric conditions, aerodynamic characteristics, propulsion system behavior, and center of gravity dynamics. The implementation incorporates penalty functions to enforce operational constraints including Mach number guidance and throttle lever position limits.

Validation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of the methodology through sensitivity analyses and comparison with reference mission profiles. Results indicate significant improvements in fuel efficiency while maintaining operational feasibility and constraint satisfaction.

The developed framework provides a foundation for advanced mission analysis and trajectory planning applications in aircraft design and operations.
