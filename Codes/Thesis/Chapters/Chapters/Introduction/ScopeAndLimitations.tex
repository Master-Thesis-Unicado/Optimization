\section{Scope and Limitations}

This section delineates the boundaries of the research and identifies key assumptions and limitations that define the applicability of the developed methodology.

\subsection{Scope}

The research focuses on trajectory optimization for fixed-wing aircraft operating in subsonic and transonic flight regimes. The methodology addresses complete mission profiles encompassing climb, cruise, and descent phases, with particular emphasis on fuel consumption minimization as the primary optimization objective.

The framework is designed for concept design applications where rapid iteration and evaluation of multiple mission configurations are required. The optimization considers three-dimensional state spaces including altitude, Mach number, and throttle lever position, accounting for dynamic mass evolution throughout mission execution.

The methodology incorporates standard atmospheric models, aerodynamic relationships, and propulsion system characteristics typical of commercial transport aircraft. Operational constraints including flight envelope limits, terminal state requirements, and throttle lever position restrictions are explicitly modeled within the optimization framework.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations and assumptions constrain the applicability of the developed methodology:

\textbf{Atmospheric Modeling:} The framework employs standard atmospheric models (ISA) and does not account for weather effects, wind variations, or atmospheric disturbances. The optimization assumes static atmospheric conditions throughout mission execution.

\textbf{Aircraft Modeling:} The methodology employs point-mass aircraft dynamics, neglecting rotational dynamics, structural flexibility, and detailed aerodynamic interactions. Aerodynamic characteristics are represented through simplified models that may not capture all nonlinear effects present in actual flight operations.

\textbf{Operational Constraints:} The framework incorporates a subset of operational constraints relevant to trajectory optimization. Additional constraints such as airspace restrictions, traffic separation requirements, and air traffic control procedures are not explicitly modeled.

\textbf{Computational Considerations:} The dynamic programming approach requires discretization of continuous state spaces, introducing approximation errors that depend on grid resolution. Computational requirements scale with state space dimensionality and discretization density, potentially limiting applicability to very high-fidelity scenarios requiring extremely fine resolution.

\textbf{Validation:} The methodology is validated through comparison with reference mission profiles and sensitivity analyses. Direct validation against flight test data or high-fidelity simulation tools may reveal additional limitations not captured in the current validation framework.

\subsection{Assumptions}

The following assumptions underpin the methodology:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Aircraft operates within standard flight envelope limits throughout mission execution
    \item Fuel consumption follows modeled relationships without accounting for fuel system dynamics or fuel sloshing effects
    \item Propulsion system characteristics are accurately represented by the employed models
    \item Atmospheric conditions remain constant throughout mission execution
    \item Center of gravity variations due to fuel consumption follow modeled relationships
    \item Operational procedures can be represented through the implemented constraint formulations
\end{itemize}

These assumptions enable tractable optimization problem formulation while maintaining sufficient fidelity for concept design applications. The limitations and assumptions should be considered when interpreting results and applying the methodology to specific design scenarios.
