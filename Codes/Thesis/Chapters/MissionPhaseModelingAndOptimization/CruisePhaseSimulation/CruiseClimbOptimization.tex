Level cruise flight satisfies equilibrium conditions: lift equals weight ($L = W$) and thrust equals drag ($T = D$). At constant altitude and Mach number, these conditions determine the required thrust setting and associated fuel consumption rate.

The equilibrium thrust requirement follows directly from the drag model:

\begin{equation}
T_{required} = D(M_{cruise}, h_{cruise}, m)
\end{equation}

As fuel consumption reduces mass, drag diminishes due to reduced induced drag, requiring progressively less thrust to maintain level flight. Throttle lever modulation achieves this thrust reduction by solving for the lever position $\delta$ that satisfies $T(\delta, M_{cruise}, h_{cruise}) = D(M_{cruise}, h_{cruise}, m)$.

For extended missions, an optional cruise-climb capability enables step-wise altitude increases during cruise to maintain near-optimal performance as mass decreases. The cruise-climb procedure divides the cruise phase into segments: initial level cruise, climb segment via dynamic programming, and continued level cruise at the higher altitude. The climb segment employs the same 3D dynamic programming framework used for the main climb phase, optimized to maintain approximately constant Mach number while gaining altitude. The altitude increment $\Delta h_{cruise}$ and trigger distance fraction are configurable parameters, allowing customization of the cruise-climb strategy based on mission requirements.
