The descent optimization objective is to minimize total fuel consumption from cruise altitude $h_{cruise}$ to target approach altitude $h_{approach}$ while satisfying the terminal Mach constraint. The problem formulation is:

\begin{equation}
\min_{M(h), \delta(h)} \int_{h_{cruise}}^{h_{approach}} \frac{\dot{m}(h, M, \delta)}{|P_s(h, M, \delta)|} \, dh
\end{equation}

subject to constraints:

\begin{equation}
P_s(h, M, \delta) < 0
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
M(h_{approach}) \in [M_{target} - \epsilon, M_{target} + \epsilon]
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
M_{min}(h, m) \leq M(h) \leq M_{MMO}
\end{equation}

The absolute value $|P_s|$ appears in the cost function denominator since $P_s$ is negative for descent. This formulation maintains the same cost density interpretation as climb: fuel consumed per unit altitude change.
