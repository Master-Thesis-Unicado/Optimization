
Time and distance are computed through post-processing of the optimal state sequence following trajectory extraction. For each segment from $h_k$ to $h_{k+1}$, the time increment is:

\begin{equation}
\Delta t_k = \frac{\Delta h_k}{P_{s,k}}
\end{equation}

where $P_{s,k}$ denotes the specific excess power at the segment, computed using average conditions between states $k$ and $k+1$:

\begin{equation}
P_{s,k} = \frac{(T_k - D_k) V_k}{m_k g}
\end{equation}

with $T_k$, $D_k$, $V_k$, and $m_k$ evaluated at average altitude $\bar{h}_k = (h_k + h_{k+1})/2$, average Mach $\bar{M}_k = (M_k + M_{k+1})/2$, and average weight $\bar{m}_k = (m_k + m_{k+1})/2$ for the segment.

Ground distance follows from integrating true airspeed over time. For each segment:

\begin{equation}
\Delta s_k = V_{TAS,k} \cdot \Delta t_k = \bar{M}_k \cdot a(\bar{h}_k) \cdot \Delta t_k
\end{equation}

where $V_{TAS,k} = \bar{M}_k \cdot a(\bar{h}_k)$ is the true airspeed computed using average conditions for the segment. The small-angle approximation assumes the horizontal velocity component approximately equals the true airspeed for typical climb angles. Summing segment distances throughout the climb profile yields the cumulative distance:

\begin{equation}
s_k = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \Delta s_i
\end{equation}

The trajectory reconstruction phase performs this post-processing, computing time and fuel arrays from the optimal state sequence using segment-wise integration procedures.
