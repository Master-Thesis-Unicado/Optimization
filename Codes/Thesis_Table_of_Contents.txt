CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Overview of aircraft trajectory optimization and mission analysis. Introduces the research objectives, scope, and structure of the thesis work. Presents the motivation for developing an integrated mission analysis framework capable of optimizing fuel consumption across all flight phases.

1.1. Research Motivation and Background
   The aviation industry faces increasing pressure to minimize operational costs and environmental impact. This section discusses the significance of trajectory optimization in reducing fuel consumption and emissions during commercial flight operations.

1.2. Problem Statement
   Traditional mission analysis methods often optimize flight phases independently, leading to suboptimal global solutions. This work addresses the need for integrated multi-phase trajectory optimization considering dynamic mass, varying atmospheric conditions, and operational constraints.

1.3. Research Objectives
   Primary objective is to develop a comprehensive mission analysis system employing three-dimensional dynamic programming for climb and descent optimization, coupled with cruise simulation and mission-level optimization algorithms.

1.4. Thesis Structure and Organization
   Overview of the thesis organization, outlining the progression from theoretical foundations through implementation to validation and results analysis.


CHAPTER 2: THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Establishes the mathematical and physical principles underlying aircraft trajectory optimization. Presents the fundamental equations of motion, atmospheric modeling, propulsion theory, and aerodynamic relationships essential for mission analysis.

2.1. Aircraft Equations of Motion
   Derivation of three-degree-of-freedom point-mass dynamics governing aircraft trajectory. Includes the energy-state approximation and specific excess power formulation fundamental to climb and descent performance analysis.
  2.1.1. Point-Mass Dynamics Formulation
     Force balance equations and kinematic relationships for trajectory modeling.
  2.1.2. Energy-State Approximation
     Transformation to energy coordinates for simplified trajectory optimization.
  2.1.3. Specific Excess Power
     Definition and physical interpretation of Ps = (T-D)V/(mg) as the fundamental performance metric.

2.2. Atmospheric Modeling
   International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model implementation for temperature, pressure, and density as functions of altitude. Essential for accurate aerodynamic and propulsion calculations.
  2.2.1. ISA Layer Structure
     Troposphere, tropopause, and stratosphere layer definitions with governing equations.
  2.2.2. Thermodynamic Properties
     Temperature lapse rates, polytropic relations, and speed of sound calculations.
  2.2.3. Density Altitude Effects
     Air density variation impact on aerodynamic forces and engine performance.

2.3. Aerodynamic Modeling
   Comprehensive treatment of aircraft aerodynamic characteristics including lift and drag computation via trim analysis, polar curves, and coefficient interpolation from tabulated data.
  2.3.1. Lift and Drag Forces
     Fundamental force equations D = CD·q·S and L = CL·q·S where q represents dynamic pressure.
  2.3.2. Trim Analysis and Equilibrium
     Longitudinal equilibrium conditions determining angle of attack and horizontal tail deflection.
  2.3.3. Drag Polar and Mass Dependency
     Induced drag variation with aircraft weight and configuration-dependent drag components.
  2.3.4. Flight Envelope Constraints
     Maximum operating Mach number (MMO), stall boundaries, and structural limits defining feasible flight region.

2.4. Propulsion System Modeling
   Turbofan engine performance modeling including thrust generation and fuel consumption characteristics across the operational envelope.
  2.4.1. Thrust Production Mechanics
     Engine thrust as a function of throttle lever position, Mach number, and altitude: T(δ,M,h).
  2.4.2. Thrust-Specific Fuel Consumption
     TSFC modeling and its variation with operating conditions, defining fuel flow rate ṁ = TSFC·T.
  2.4.3. Throttle Lever Dynamics
     Relationship between lever position and engine operating regimes including idle, cruise, and maximum thrust settings.

2.5. Aircraft Mass and Center of Gravity
   Dynamic mass evolution during flight due to fuel consumption and its impact on aerodynamic trim, performance, and stability.
  2.5.1. Mass Breakdown and Components
     Operating empty weight, payload, and fuel mass components comprising total aircraft mass.
  2.5.2. Fuel Tank Configuration
     Multi-tank fuel system architecture with individual tank capacities and center of gravity positions.
  2.5.3. Dynamic CG Calculation
     Longitudinal CG position computation as weighted average: x_CG = Σ(mi·xi)/Σmi during fuel depletion.
  2.5.4. Fuel Consumption Scenarios
     Sequential tank depletion strategies (outer-first, center-first, proportional) affecting CG trajectory.


CHAPTER 3: DYNAMIC PROGRAMMING OPTIMIZATION THEORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Comprehensive treatment of dynamic programming methodology applied to trajectory optimization. Covers Bellman's principle of optimality, discrete state-space formulation, and computational implementation strategies.

3.1. Fundamentals of Dynamic Programming
   Introduction to dynamic programming as a method for solving multi-stage decision problems by decomposing them into overlapping subproblems.
  3.1.1. Principle of Optimality
     Bellman's principle stating that optimal policies have the property that remaining decisions must be optimal with respect to the state resulting from the first decision.
  3.1.2. Stage-Based Decomposition
     Partitioning the trajectory optimization problem into sequential altitude stages.
  3.1.3. Cost-to-Go Functions
     Definition of J(x,k) as the minimum cost from state x at stage k to the terminal condition.

3.2. Three-Dimensional State Space Formulation
   Extension of classical 2D dynamic programming to 3D state space (altitude, Mach, throttle) for comprehensive trajectory control.
  3.2.1. State Variables and Discretization
     Definition of state vector X = (h,M,δ) and grid discretization strategies balancing accuracy and computational cost.
  3.2.2. Control Variable Integration
     Incorporation of throttle lever position as a third state dimension enabling thrust optimization.
  3.2.3. Feasible Region Definition
     Constraints defining admissible states based on flight envelope, stall boundaries, and operational limits.

3.3. Cost Function Formulation
   Development of the fuel consumption cost functional serving as the optimization objective.
  3.3.1. Fuel-per-Distance Metric
     Derivation of J = ṁ/Ps as fuel cost density [kg/m] representing fuel consumed per unit altitude change.
  3.3.2. Trapezoidal Integration Scheme
     Numerical integration of cost functional over altitude segments using trapezoidal rule.
  3.3.3. Mass-Coupled Cost Evaluation
     Iterative cost computation accounting for dynamic mass reduction affecting drag and specific excess power.

3.4. Bellman Recursion and Solution Algorithm
   Forward dynamic programming algorithm implementation solving the discrete optimization problem via recursive cost propagation.
  3.4.1. Forward Recursion Scheme
     Stage-by-stage cost propagation from initial to terminal conditions.
  3.4.2. Transition Kernel Design
     Definition of allowable state transitions between consecutive stages using 7×7 grid neighborhoods.
  3.4.3. Optimal Path Backtracking
     Reconstruction of optimal trajectory by following predecessor links from terminal to initial state.

3.5. Penalty Functions and Soft Constraints
   Augmentation of cost function with penalty terms to guide solutions toward operationally feasible and efficient trajectories.
  3.5.1. Mach Trajectory Guidance
     Progressive penalty system ensuring reachability of target terminal Mach number through reachability corridors.
  3.5.2. Throttle Lever Penalties
     Penalties discouraging sustained high-thrust operation to reflect engine operational limits and wear considerations.
  3.5.3. Penalty Weighting and Tuning
     Calibration methodology for penalty coefficients balancing guidance effectiveness and optimality preservation.

3.6. Computational Complexity and Performance
   Analysis of algorithmic complexity and computational optimization strategies enabling practical implementation.
  3.6.1. Complexity Analysis
     Time complexity O(K·I·L) and space complexity scaling with grid dimensions.
  3.6.2. Caching and Memoization
     Performance optimization through pre-computation and caching of engine and aerodynamic evaluations.
  3.6.3. Grid Resolution Trade-offs
     Balance between solution accuracy and computational cost through grid density selection.


CHAPTER 4: MISSION PHASE MODELING AND OPTIMIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Detailed development of computational methods for each flight phase: climb, cruise, and descent. Presents phase-specific optimization formulations, integration schemes, and performance computation procedures.

4.1. Climb Phase Optimization
   Application of 3D dynamic programming to minimum-fuel climb trajectory determination from takeoff to cruise altitude.
  4.1.1. Climb Problem Formulation
     Objective: minimize total fuel from h₀ to h_cruise while reaching target Mach M_target with specified tolerance.
  4.1.2. Initial and Terminal Conditions
     Specification of takeoff state (altitude, velocity, throttle) and cruise entry requirements.
  4.1.3. 3D DP Implementation
     Grid construction over altitude schedule with Mach and lever discretization, forward pass execution, and path extraction.
  4.1.4. Time and Distance Integration
     Post-processing to compute temporal evolution and ground distance from optimal altitude-Mach profile.
  4.1.5. Alternative Climb Strategies
     Comparison with conventional strategies: constant CAS/Mach, maximum rate of climb, and energy-based methods.

4.2. Cruise Phase Simulation
   Level-flight cruise simulation at constant altitude and Mach number, with optional cruise-climb capability for extended missions.
  4.2.1. Steady-State Cruise Dynamics
     Equilibrium conditions for level flight: L=W and T=D, yielding required thrust and fuel flow profiles.
  4.2.2. Time-Stepping Integration
     Explicit Euler integration of fuel consumption and distance accumulation over specified cruise range.
  4.2.3. Cruise-Climb Optimization
     Step-climb procedure using DP optimization at discrete intervals to maintain near-optimal cruise altitude as mass decreases.
  4.2.4. Cruise Performance Metrics
     Fuel flow rate, specific range, and lift-to-drag ratio evolution throughout cruise segment.

4.3. Descent Phase Optimization
   Application of 3D dynamic programming to minimum-fuel descent trajectory from cruise altitude to approach conditions.
  4.3.1. Descent Problem Formulation
     Objective: minimize fuel consumption during descent from h_cruise to h_approach while satisfying terminal Mach constraint.
  4.3.2. Stall Constraint Handling
     Dynamic minimum Mach calculation accounting for decreasing altitude and varying mass to maintain stall margin.
  4.3.3. Idle-Thrust Descent Considerations
     Modeling of flight idle thrust settings and their impact on descent profile optimization.
  4.3.4. 3D DP for Descent
     Backward-in-altitude optimization with descent-specific penalty tuning and grid configuration.
  4.3.5. Descent Rate and Time Computation
     Calculation of vertical speed profile and total descent duration from optimal trajectory.

4.4. Mission Integration and Continuity
   Procedures ensuring consistent state transitions between flight phases and maintaining physical continuity.
  4.4.1. Phase Transition States
     Matching climb terminal conditions to cruise initial state and cruise terminal to descent initial state.
  4.4.2. Cumulative Fuel Accounting
     Tracking total mission fuel consumption across all phases with mass propagation.
  4.4.3. Time and Distance Aggregation
     Summation of phase-wise duration and ground distance for total mission metrics.


CHAPTER 5: MISSION-LEVEL OPTIMIZATION ALGORITHMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Higher-level optimization procedures for fuel capacity minimization and mission range targeting. Presents iterative algorithms operating on complete mission simulations.

5.1. Fuel Capacity Optimization
   Determination of minimum required fuel load to complete specified mission profile with safety margins.
  5.1.1. Bisection Method Formulation
     Binary search algorithm guaranteeing monotonic convergence to minimum feasible fuel capacity.
  5.1.2. Fuel Deficit Function
     Definition of deficit metric quantifying shortfall or surplus relative to consumption requirements.
  5.1.3. Convergence Criteria
     Tolerance-based termination conditions and maximum iteration limits.
  5.1.4. Safety Buffer Application
     Addition of operational reserves (typically 5%) to converged minimum fuel value.
  5.1.5. Iteration History and Analysis
     Tracking convergence behavior and fuel bound evolution throughout optimization.

5.2. Mission Range Optimization
   Iterative adjustment of cruise distance to achieve target total mission range considering climb and descent distance contributions.
  5.2.1. Range Targeting Problem
     Objective: determine cruise distance s_cruise such that s_total = s_climb + s_cruise + s_descent equals target range.
  5.2.2. Distance Integration Methods
     True airspeed integration over time for each phase: s = ∫V_TAS·dt with small-angle approximation.
  5.2.3. Damped Iterative Scheme
     Proportional adjustment algorithm with damping factor preventing oscillation: s_cruise,k+1 = s_cruise,k + α·error.
  5.2.4. Convergence Acceleration
     Adaptive damping and extrapolation techniques improving convergence rate.
  5.2.5. Multi-Objective Considerations
     Combined fuel-range optimization potential and trade-off analysis.

5.3. Configuration Management
   Systematic parameter management and dynamic reconfiguration enabling optimization iterations.
  5.3.1. Parameter Update Mechanisms
     Runtime modification of fuel capacity and cruise distance parameters between iterations.
  5.3.2. State Preservation and Restoration
     Saving and loading optimal trajectories for analysis and comparison.
